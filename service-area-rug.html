The problem

Surface vacuuming never reaches the base.

Rugs trap grit between the pile and the foundation. Walking on that grit wears fibers from the inside. Vacuuming the top looks fine for a week—then the dull look returns.

Pet hair, tracked-in soil, and spills settle deeper than a home vacuum reaches. Area rugs also need a different approach than wall-to-wall carpet cleaning because they can be moved, inspected from both sides, and dried with more control.

Materials matter

Fiber and construction change the process

Not every rug should be cleaned the same way. We adjust based on what the rug is made of and how it is built—not a single foam or spray for every piece.

Wool rugs

Wool holds soil and can be sensitive to over-wetting and harsh alkalinity. We treat wool with extra care around moisture and chemistry.

Synthetic rugs

Nylon, olefin, and other synthetics are common in homes and often tolerate thorough cleaning well when dye stability is acceptable.

Delicate or specialty pieces

Older, hand-finished, or fragile rugs may need a gentler approach—or a limit on what cleaning can safely achieve. We say so before work starts.

Color and fringe

Color bleeding and fringe damage are real risks on some rugs. We check colorfastness concerns and handle fringes carefully rather than forcing an aggressive clean.

How we work

Fiber-aware cleaning, not one-size foam

We identify construction and fiber as far as inspection allows, then choose the process: controlled extraction, gentle agitation, or a more conservative clean for delicate pieces—so color and texture are respected while soil is removed.

Fiber identification

What the rug is made of drives moisture level, chemistry, and how much mechanical action is safe.

Deep soil removal

The goal is soil that vacuuming leaves behind—not only surface dust.

Odor and stain treatment

Pet and spill issues on rugs can be addressed as part of the clean when appropriate. Severe odor may also relate to our pet odor and stain service.

Careful drying

Rugs need to dry thoroughly. We explain expected dry time so the rug is not returned to service while damp.

Good to know

Area rug cleaning questions

Can wool rugs be cleaned?

Yes, many wool rugs can be cleaned successfully with a fiber-aware approach. Wool needs controlled moisture and appropriate chemistry; we assess condition first.

Can synthetic rugs be cleaned?

Yes. Synthetic rugs are common and often respond well to thorough cleaning when dyes are stable and the backing is sound.

How do you handle color bleeding?

We watch for colorfastness concerns during inspection and testing when needed. If a rug is likely to bleed, we discuss limits before proceeding rather than forcing a process that could worsen it.

How long does a rug take to dry?

Dry time depends on fiber, thickness, humidity, and airflow. We give guidance for your specific rug so it is not put back in place while damp.

Is every rug cleaned the same way?

No. Fiber, construction, and condition determine the method. Delicate or damaged rugs may need a gentler clean—or may not be good candidates for aggressive washing.

Is this the same as wall-to-wall carpet cleaning?

Related tools and chemistry, different workflow. Rugs can be handled as individual pieces; installed wall-to-wall carpet is covered on our carpet cleaning service page.
